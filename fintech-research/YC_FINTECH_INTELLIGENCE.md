**Interpretation (opinion):** YC's 2026 fintech thesis centres on two things:
- **Stablecoin-based money movement for global businesses**, especially emerging markets.
- **AI doing operational finance work** (back office, compliance, credit review).

Cross-border business payments into and out of emerging markets sit at the intersection of both.

## 3. Investment patterns (separating fact from interpretation)

| Question | Fact or interpretation | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| What is attracting investment? | Fact (RFS and batch) | Stablecoin money movement, agent payments/identity, AI compliance agents, AI back office for banks/lenders |
| Becoming commoditized | Interpretation | KYC document extraction, generic AML alert triage, AI bookkeeping for US SMBs, "Stripe for agents" (Stripe, Visa, Mastercard and Coinbase are all shipping agent-payment products, S10–S12) |
| Crowded | Interpretation | Agent payments: Skyfire, Payman, Crossmint, Nekuda, Natural, Stripe ACS, Visa Intelligent Commerce, Mastercard Agent Pay, x402 (S10–S12). Reported x402 volume was around $28k/day, mostly testing (S11). |
| Under-served | Interpretation | Non-US regulatory workflows (e.g. Moroccan/North African tax and payment-terms rules), emerging-market SME working capital, and trade documentation outside OECD markets |
| Where manual labour remains | Interpretation | Regulatory declarations in emerging markets, trade-document checks, supplier-payment reconciliation across local banks |

**Caution:** the number of YC companies in a category is not demand, and is not saturation.

## 4. Implication for this team

Building a US agent-payments startup means competing with Visa, Stripe and Coinbase on day one. YC also funds **emerging-market operators with a local regulatory edge**: the credit-review-for-EM-lenders company and Aspora/DolarApp are examples. A Moroccan, regulation-driven finance-ops company fits that pattern better than a me-too US product.
